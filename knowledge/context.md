# Reference context

This file is stored separately from the decision procedure in `knowledge/methodology.md`. It is a supplied synthesis of price-action notes associated with The Candlestick Trading Bible and Simple Trading Book. It is not a verbatim textbook, and it does not decide the verdict. The decision procedure decides the verdict. Use this file only for definitions of trend, level, candlestick signals, confluence, and risk arithmetic.

## Core framework

Every setup must satisfy trend, level, and signal before execution. Trend is the dominant directional state. Level is a zone where supply and demand meet or structure turns. Signal is the candle or multi-bar pattern that times the execution. Trades aligned with the dominant trend are the higher-probability cases.

## Market structure

Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows. Draw support along swing lows.

Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows. Draw resistance along swing highs.

Range: price oscillates between horizontal support and resistance, with at least two touches on each boundary.

Read the higher timeframe first for major structure and supply or demand. Read the execution timeframe only at those levels. A bullish signal directly under major higher-timeframe resistance is low probability. A bearish signal directly above major higher-timeframe support is low probability.

## Candlestick signals

Pin bar or hammer: small real body at the top and a lower wick at least twice the body. Bullish rejection at support or a market low.

Shooting star or bearish pin: small real body at the bottom and an upper wick at least twice the body. Bearish rejection at resistance or a market high.

Dragonfly doji: open, high, and close nearly equal, with a long lower tail. Rejection at support.

Gravestone doji: open, low, and close nearly equal, with a long upper tail. Rejection at resistance.

Engulfing bar: the second real body completely covers the previous real body. Momentum shift at a key level or a trend pullback.

Harami or inside bar: a small body fully inside the prior candle's high-low range. Contraction before expansion. Also the setup for a false-break trap.

Morning star: large down candle, small indecision candle, then a large up candle closing more than halfway into the first candle. Bullish transition at support.

Evening star: large up candle, small indecision candle, then a large down candle closing more than halfway into the first candle. Bearish transition at resistance.

Tweezers: two or more candles with matching highs or matching lows. Repeated failure at a ceiling or floor.

If the wick-to-body ratio or the close cannot be read, the pattern is NOT VISIBLE. Do not invent it.

## Chart formations

Double or triple top, and double or triple bottom: repeated tests, then a neckline break or a retest of that break.

Head and shoulders, and inverse head and shoulders: entry only when price breaks the neckline.

Flags: a counter-trend channel after a strong move. Entry on the channel break.

Triangles and wedges: wait for the boundary break and, when the method requires it, the retest. A falling wedge breaking up and a rising wedge breaking down are the usual resolutions, only if that break is visible.

Harmonic projections such as AB=CD are estimates. Mark them "(est.)" and do not treat them as a visible axis level.

## Confluence and entry

A high-probability setup needs at least two of: dominant higher-timeframe trend, a static key level, a supply or demand origin, or a dynamic level that is actually drawn.

Dynamic levels count only when drawn: a 21-period moving average, a Bollinger band, or a 50% or 61.8% retracement. If it is not on the image, it is NOT VISIBLE.

Aggressive entry is the close of the signal candle. Conservative entry is the 50% retracement of that candle, which shortens risk. Breakout entry waits for the break and a retest with a signal candle.

## Risk arithmetic

Risk at most 1% to 2% of equity per trade. The decision procedure is stricter: 1% only for A+, 0.5% for B, otherwise no trade.

Minimum reward to risk in these notes is 1:2, and 1:3 is preferred. The decision procedure requires T2 of at least 1:3 or the verdict is stand aside.

Stop for a long is beyond the signal or zone low. Stop for a short is beyond the signal or zone high. The decision procedure adds the inducement-wick buffer. Targets sit at the next opposing structure, not at an invented round number.

## Liquidity traps

Obvious highs, lows, and inside-bar boundaries collect stops. A false break that sweeps those stops and closes back into the range is a trap. The trade, if the decision procedure allows it, is in the opposite direction of that false break, and only after the sweep and the required confirmation. A sweep alone is not an entry.
